### How Search Engines Actually Work

**Search Engines Find Information via Crawlers and Indexing**

- Crawlers/Spiders constantly scan the internet, discovering and reading webpages.
- These pages get added to a giant **Index** — a mapping between search terms and webpages.
- When you search something (e.g., "What is Thermodynamics"), the search engine looks through this index, ranks the most relevant documents, and returns results.

**Search Engines Keep Updating**

- Crawlers are always searching for new content, keeping the index fresh.
- Example: If a major event happens, related news articles get crawled quickly — which is why fresh news shows up in search soon after.

**How Search Engines Rank Documents**

- **Domain Authority** — a page from a trusted, well-known site (e.g., Wikipedia) tends to rank higher than a lesser-known new site.
- **Page Speed** — faster-loading pages rank higher.
- **Keywords** — relevance of content to the search term.
- **Average time spent** by users on the page — longer time signals a more useful page.
- **Backlinks** — other sites linking to yours signals trust/relevance.
- **Meta tags and publish date** are also factored in.

**Important Things to Remember About Search Engines**

- Flaws: Search engines do **not** guarantee truth. Rankings can be outdated. Results can be misleading.
- How to verify: Trace back to the original source, check who published it, check the date, compare multiple results.

### How LLMs Actually Generate Answers

**How LLMs Generate Text**

- An LLM works by predicting the next most likely word/text based on patterns learned during training — it doesn't "know" facts the way a search engine retrieves them.
- A modern LLM uses a large context window and has learned extremely complex patterns across grammar, programming, reasoning, facts, language, slang, math, events, places, and associations between concepts.
- Example: Ask "What is the capital of India?" → the model predicts "Delhi" with very high probability (e.g., 99%) because during training, this pattern appeared correctly the vast majority of times it was seen.

**What Knowledge Does an LLM Actually Contain?**

- A trained neural network contains a huge collection of numbers called **parameters (or weights)**.
- During training, these numbers are repeatedly adjusted until the model gets good at predicting the next word correctly.

**Knowledge Cutoff**

- Every LLM has a Knowledge Cutoff date — the point up to which its training data goes.
- The neural network isn't retrained automatically every day — it doesn't learn new things on its own after training ends.

**Base Model vs AI Assistant**

- A **Base Model** is trained primarily to predict/continue text (e.g., given "Once upon a time...", it just continues the story).
- A base model has no built-in security or tool access — if asked something harmful (e.g., how to hack a WiFi password), it may answer without hesitation.
- An **AI Assistant** (like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) is built on top of a base model, with additional layers added:
  - Web Search, Files, Memory
  - Instruction Tuning, System Instructions
  - Tool Access, Retrieval
  - Guard Rails, Security, Auth, Content Filters
  - Conversation Management
  - Human Feedback, Safety Training

**Inference vs Training**

- **Inference** is the process of giving a prompt to an AI assistant and getting back a response — this happens every time you chat with it (as opposed to Training, which is the earlier process of building the model itself).

### Why AI Sounds So Confident (Even When Wrong)

**Why Models Confidently Produce Wrong Answers (Hallucination)**

- A **hallucination** occurs when an AI model generates information that sounds plausible but is actually unsupported, incorrect, misleading, or fabricated.
- Language quality and factual accuracy are two separate things — confident, fluent language does not mean the content is true.

**Why Hallucination Happens**

- Ambiguous prompts
- Insufficient information
- Outdated knowledge
- False assumptions
- Unreliable source information
- Models are optimized to always give an answer
- Text generation is inherently probabilistic (based on likelihood, not certainty)

**Types of Hallucination**

- Invented facts
- Invented citations
- Incorrect combination of real facts
- Outdated facts
- False precision
- Faulty boolean/logical reasoning

**The Confidence Illusion**

- Humans naturally use **tone** as a signal of truth — e.g., "I think the answer may be XYZ" feels less trustworthy than "The answer is definitely XYZ," even if both could be equally right or wrong.
- AI models tend to phrase most answers confidently, regardless of how certain they actually are — which can mislead people into over-trusting them.

**How ChatGPT Actually Works (Putting It Together)**

- ChatGPT = Web Search + LLM → i.e., **Retrieval** + **Generation**
- Retrieval gives external, real-world evidence; Generation converts that evidence into a useful, readable response.
- Important: These tools reduce errors, but do **not** completely eliminate them.

---

### Key Takeaway

Search engines retrieve real documents from an index — they don't generate anything. LLMs, on the other hand, don't "look things up"; they predict the next most likely word based on patterns learned during training. That's why AI assistants combine both: web search (retrieval) for real evidence, and an LLM (generation) to turn that evidence into a readable answer. Even so, confident-sounding text is never a guarantee of truth — always trace back to the source.
